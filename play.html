Glacier Rangers

Eleven game sheets. Each one is a short mission in the real viewer: you look, measure, bet and explain. Nobody tells you the answers; you find them in the satellite pictures. Play on screen or print the sheets.

Pick a sheet in any order. Sheet 1 is the base camp where you learn the tools. Stars show how hard a step is: ★ explorer (about 9–12 years), ★★ ranger (about 13–15), ★★★ scientist (16 and up). Points are kept in this browser only; nothing is sent anywhere.

The rules of the game

1. Look first. Every answer is something you can see or measure in the viewer. If a number is on the screen, copy it exactly.

Bet before you check

2. In the bet steps you write your guess before you look. Points for being right, and extra honesty points for writing down how sure you were.

Say what you cannot say

3. A satellite picture shows what it shows. When a cloud, shadow or snow hides the ice, write that down. “Cannot tell” is a good answer.

For teachers and parents

What each sheet trains, and how to run it. The sheets contain no glacier facts of their own: every number the players use is read from the viewer, so it is always the source's number.

    Running a session. One screen per pair works best; 40 minutes fit one or two sheets. Print the sheet for pairs without a device and let them look at one shared projector picture. For a tournament, add up the team points of sheets 3, 7 and 9. Play the debrief question of each sheet aloud: it is where the learning happens.

    Honest points. Ticks are on the honour system. Ask to see the screen for the steps marked ★★★. Predictions are scored by the page from what the players type, so a wrong guess still teaches.
