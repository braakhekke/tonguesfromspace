4 · Stakes line: water or ice equivalent? What is the “+”?

Feedback: "'GLAMOS stakes 2 stake-years (elevations 1980–3340 m): stake minus GLAMOS bin balance +1633 mm w.e. on average, minus the flight-calibrated bin balance +1633 mm w.e.' Water equivalent or ice equivalent? Do not fully get what that plus means. Is it additional?"

Today (provenance page)

GLAMOS stakes 2 stake-years (elevations 1980–3340 m): stake minus GLAMOS bin balance +1633 mm w.e. on average, minus the flight-calibrated bin balance +1633 mm w.e.

Proposed

GLAMOS stake measurements: 2 stake-years, 1980–3340 m
Stakes vs. GLAMOS bin balance
+1633 mm w.e. (average)
Stakes vs. flight-calibrated bin balance
+1633 mm w.e.

How to read it. The difference is stake minus model. A plus means the stakes recorded a higher balance (less melt) than the model for the same elevations; a minus would mean lower. It is a comparison between two values, not an extra amount. w.e. = water equivalent: the depth of liquid water the ice or snow would melt to (ice is lighter than water by a factor of about 0.9, so one metre of ice is roughly 0.9 m w.e.).

Check before building I have not yet checked the sign convention and the unit against the data (the line is built in provenance.html from stake_minus_bin and stake_minus_kappa_bin). My explanation of the plus is my reading of "stake minus bin"; it must be confirmed against the script that makes those fields. Both values being exactly +1633 also looks odd (two different bin balances giving the same difference); worth looking at as a possible bug, separate from the wording. The "0.9" density figure is the usual ice-to-water ratio; the site uses 0.85 in its code (balance / 0.85) and I would take the site's own number, labelled as such, after checking.

5 · “Hiking trails, tongue in view”: how is it computed? Trees?

Feedback: "How is the 'Hiking trails tongue in view' calculated? It looks a bit odd around Aletsch. Are trees included?"

Text behind the “i” (proposed)

Where can you see the tongue?

The violet band marks where a person 1.7 m tall could see the glacier tongue from the trail. I computed it from the terrain model alone. Trees and buildings are not counted, so in forest the tongue may be hidden although the band shows it. Treat it as a guide, not a promise.

From the codeThe answer to the question is already in the site's own About text (js/07-render.js): "my own calculation from the elevation model; trees and buildings are not counted". So the odd look around Aletsch is likely forest and the terrain model alone. The change is to put the sentence where people look (an "i" at the layer and the legend) instead of in the long About text. I have not examined the Aletsch picture itself, so I cannot say yet whether anything else is off there.
