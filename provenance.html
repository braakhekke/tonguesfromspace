Kinds of surface

  • measured: a dated DEM or lidar surface.
  • interpolated: between two measured surfaces.
  • extrapolated: beyond the last one, at its rate.
  • provisional: 2026.
  • Date: every year is the surface on 30 September of that year (the end of the glaciological year).

A measured surface is moved from its survey day to 30 September by the same model. So the surface shown for a surveyed year is not the raw survey: it is the survey surface lowered by the melt between the survey day and 30 September (for a survey in August, the weeks in between).

Sharing the loss between years

  • Solid: the GLAMOS balance sets how the loss between two measured surfaces falls into each year.
  • Dashed: extrapolated at the last segment's rate (2026 also with its own balance).
  • Not used: measured years.

Uncertainty

Median over the tongue ice, red whisker the 90th percentile. Calibrated on the same ICESat-2 shots. 2026 has none yet: it is provisional and its balance was read from the GLAMOS figure.

Independent checks

  • ICESat-2 also fitted the 2023 anchor day and σ, so it is not independent (rmse is leave-one-granule-out).
  • ASTER and stakes are checks only.

Ice bed: radar points against the bed model

Experimental outlines of the tongue

Yearly lines around the lowest 2 km of the tongue (layer "Experimental outline"). This is an experiment: not an official outline and not a measurement.

  • Who draws them. A neural network, GlaViTU (Maslov, Persello, Schellenberger and Stein, "Globally scalable glacier mapping by deep learning matches expert delineation accuracy", Nature Communications 16, 43, 2025, doi 10.1038/s41467-024-54956-x), with the weights glavitu_regional_ALP from that study. It reads the six optical Sentinel-2 bands plus height and slope, and gives every 10 m pixel a probability of glacier ice.
  • Which picture. The same Sentinel-2 scene the site shows for that year. The line is where the probability is 0.5, cleaned of small pieces.
  • Smoothing. Each line is resampled every 5 m and smoothed along its length by 20 m, to remove the 10 m pixel steps. This is cosmetic.
  • Scenes. …
  • What is shown. Only the lowest 2 km, as an open line around the tip. It fades out towards its loose ends.
  • What can go wrong. One scene is one day. Fresh snow or cloud shadow can move the line, so a year can look like an advance for that reason. Debris-covered and snow-covered ice are hard for such models. The lines were not checked against a survey: do not read yearly changes as measurements.
  • Computed here. Areas and distances come from the pixels, not from a source. The reference outline in the viewer is the official inventory outline (SGI 2023).
  • Not settled. The licences of the weights and of the height tiles are still to be read before anything like this is published.

Glacier thickness 2016 (layer)

The layer "Glacier thickness 2016" colours the glacier by ice thickness. It is the ice thickness map of Grab et al. (2021), "Ice thickness distribution of all Swiss glaciers based on extended ground-penetrating radar data and glaciological modeling", Journal of Glaciology 67(266), read from the ice thickness layer of swisstopo (GLAMOS data).

  • A model, not a measurement. Only the radar points (next chapter) are measured.
  • One state for every year. The map shows the glacier around 2016 ("2016" is a round name: the reference year differs by glacier, 2017 for Aletsch). The ice has thinned since, and the layer does not change with the year you choose.
  • Cut to the glacier. Nothing is drawn on the rock around it.

Radar points (layer)

The layer "Radar points" shows ice thickness measured by ground-penetrating radar. Source: GLAMOS, "Glacier ice thickness from ground-penetrating radar", release 2020 (doi:10.18750/icethickness.2020.r2020, CC BY 4.0). The surveys were made between 1958 and 2017, depending on the glacier.

  • Measured, with a date. These are the only thickness numbers on the site that come from a measurement. A point is the thickness in the survey year, not in the year you are looking at.
  • One dot is one cell. Each dot is the mean of the radar points inside a 50 m cell (computed here). Where a cell was measured in several years, the dot is marked and a click shows all of them.
  • In 3D. Only about ten pins are shown, one on the middle line in each stretch of the glacier, to keep the view fast.
  • Against the model. Where the radar points and the thickness layer differ, the radar points are the measurement.

Ice speed (layer)

The layer "Ice speed" shows how fast the ice surface moves, as a yearly mean. Source: ITS_LIVE annual mosaics of image pair velocities, version 2.1 (120 m, one mosaic per RGI region, 2013 to 2024), NASA MEaSUREs ITS_LIVE project, Gardner et al. (its-live.jpl.nasa.gov, CC BY 4.0). The product follows features of the ice surface between pairs of satellite images (Landsat 8/9, Sentinel-2 and Sentinel-1).

  • Copied, not made here. The values are the product's, only cut to the picture box. The map shows the year of the picture you are looking at; years without a map show nothing.
  • Slow ice is left clear. Under 5 m/yr the product cannot tell ice from rock (rock beside the glaciers reads 1 to 2 m/yr), so nothing is drawn there. Only ice inside the oldest outline is shown.
  • Surface speed. It is the speed of the surface, not of deeper ice, and a yearly mean says nothing about the season.
  • Coarse. At 120 m the mosaic misses narrow ice streams and the last tens of metres of a thin tongue.

Super-resolution layer (3D view)

The optional layer "Super-resolution" in the 3D view shows the Sentinel-2 picture of a year after the super-resolution model SEN2SR (tacofoundation, model card licence cc0-1.0) has been run over it: 10 m pixels become 2.5 m pixels. It exists for the years 2016 to 2026 of the ten Swiss glaciers of this site that have such a picture; other years show the normal picture.

  • An estimate, not a measurement. The extra detail is the model's guess. The satellite did not see it. Do not read crevasses, boulders or debris edges off this layer.
  • Off by default. Every other picture on the site is the original Sentinel-2 image, never sharpened. No number on the site is taken from this layer.
  • Credit. Contains modified Copernicus Sentinel data.
