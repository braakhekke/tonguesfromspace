1Study area: the tongue of Grosser Aletsch

The tongue = the part of Grosser Aletsch within 1500 m of the centreline, from the 1984 tip to 8 km above the 2016 tip. About 10.9 km² of ice (computed, SGI2016 outline, 20 m cells). The surfaces cover the whole 3D strip (outlined in black); the model was built and checked on the lowest 8 km of the tongue (red dashed). The rest of the strip, 39 % of the modelled ice cells (19,538 of 49,849, computed), follows the same rules but has no ICESat-2 check. Pictures of years before 2016 use the 2016 surface.

Kinds of surface

measured: a dated DEM or lidar surface. interpolated: between two measured surfaces. extrapolated: beyond the last one, at its rate. provisional: 2026.

Sharing the loss between years

5Ice bed: radar points against the bed model

Three checks on Grosser Aletsch (all computed here, not source figures). The bed is the rock under the ice. "Radar bed" is the radar's own bed elevation (the survey surface minus the measured thickness, from the GLAMOS release); "model bed" is swissBEDROCK, which under glaciers is swissALTI3D minus a modelled GLAMOS ice thickness.

1 · Radar bed against model bed

At the 2,603 radar cells, how far apart are the two beds?

Median about 0 m in every survey year: −0.2 m (2011, 1,679 cells), −0.6 m (2009, 765), +1.1 m (1958 seismic, 159). Spread (standard deviation) 7 to 12 m; 81 % of cells within 10 m, 99 % within 30 m.

Reading: close, but not a validation. The model was made from these same surveys, and at the survey lines its bed is the measured bed (Grab et al. 2021, where the survey surface agreed with a DEM).

2 · 2009 against 2011

Where both years surveyed the same spot, how well do they repeat?

At 558 spots (25 m cells) the beds implied by the two surveys differ with a standard deviation of 17.9 m, and only 52 % agree within 10 m. The thickness change 2009 to 2011 has a median of −3.0 m (482 cells of 50 m) against a spread of 20.5 m.

Reading: single radar cells are noisy at the scale of tens of metres; only medians mean anything.

Each dot is one radar point of 2009 or 2011 on the lower tongue, in ground that is ice free in the newest swissALTI3D. Light blue: ice in the SGI2023 outline; dashed: the SGI2016 outline, the ice the model was made for. The points lie in the strip between the two outlines, kept 30 m clear of both. They come from only 5 places along the margin, where flight lines cross it, so the 142 points are not 142 independent samples.

What this supports

  • At the margins the model bed sits within a few metres of the exposed ground. The radar bed lies about 7 m (a tenth of the thickness) above it.
  • So at the margins the radar thickness reads too small, or the survey's surface height too high, or both. The data cannot separate the two. The helicopter surveys take the surface from GPS minus the radar-measured air layer and check it against a DEM "when an up-to-date" one exists (Rutishauser et al. 2016); which DEM, is not stated.
  • On the section chart: the dashed model bed is the line to read, the radar dots are measurements that carry this offset.

What it does not show

  • Few places: the 142 points come from 5 crossings of the margin by flight lines (3, 32, 66, 37 and 4 points), so the medians rest on a handful of locations.
  • Not the deep tongue: these are margin points, about 80 m of ice; deeper ice may behave differently.
  • Not a model error band: the model's own uncertainty maps are not in the GLAMOS release, and swissBEDROCK's uncertainty band under glaciers is about 0 (not usable). The model is also called "a maximum estimate" of the bed by its release notes.
  • Not screened: lakes and debris on the exposed ground, and positions at the margin (a few metres of horizontal error).

How the ice-free points were chosen

  • On the tongue, at least 30 m outside the SGI2023 outline (acquired 2023, all glaciers) and at least 30 m inside the SGI2016 outline, so the model had ice there. Outside SGI2016 the model bed equals the ground by construction and proves nothing.
  • Newest swissALTI3D edition, ground slope at the point at most 20°. With up to 30°: 195 points, the same result (radar +6.4 m, model +0.8 m). With a 50 m margin only 12 points remain (radar +7.8 m, model −2.1 m); with 100 m none.
