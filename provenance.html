Kinds of surface

  • measured: a dated DEM or lidar surface.
  • interpolated: between two measured surfaces.
  • extrapolated: beyond the last one, at its rate.
  • provisional: 2026.
  • Date: every year is the surface on 30 September of that year (the end of the glaciological year).

A measured surface is moved from its survey day to 30 September by the same model. So the surface shown for a surveyed year is not the raw survey: it is the survey surface lowered by the melt between the survey day and 30 September (for a survey in August, the weeks in between).

Sharing the loss between years

  • Solid: the GLAMOS balance sets how the loss between two measured surfaces falls into each year.
  • Dashed: extrapolated at the last segment's rate (2026 also with its own balance).
  • Not used: measured years.

Uncertainty

Median over the tongue ice, red whisker the 90th percentile. Calibrated on the same ICESat-2 shots. 2026 has none yet: it is provisional and its balance was read from the GLAMOS figure.

Independent checks

  • ICESat-2 also fitted the 2023 anchor day and σ, so it is not independent (rmse is leave-one-granule-out).
  • ASTER and stakes are checks only.

Experimental outlines of the tongue

The yearly lines of the lowest 2 km of the tongue in the main viewer (layer "Experimental outline") are an experiment, not an official outline and not a measurement. They are drawn by a neural network, GlaViTU (Maslov, Persello, Schellenberger and Stein, "Globally scalable glacier mapping by deep learning matches expert delineation accuracy", Nature Communications 16, 43, 2025, doi 10.1038/s41467-024-54956-x), with the weights glavitu_regional_ALP from that study. The model gets the six optical Sentinel-2 bands and a height model (heights and slope) and returns, for each 10 m pixel, a probability of glacier ice.

  • The picture. For each year the model runs on the same Sentinel-2 scene that the site shows for that year, cut to the tongue with a margin; the outline is the 0.5 probability line, cleaned of small pieces.
  • Smoothing. The 10 m pixel steps are smoothed away: each outline is resampled every 5 m and smoothed along its length by 20 m (Gaussian). This is cosmetic; the raw lines stay as they are in the experiment.
  • Scenes. …
  • The line. Only the lowest 2 km of the tongue is kept, as an open line around the tip. It fades out towards its loose ends on the map.
  • What can go wrong. One scene per year means one day: fresh snow on the tongue or cloud shadow makes the model move the line, and a year can look like an advance for that reason. Debris-covered and snow-covered ice are the hard cases for such models. The lines were not checked against a survey, and yearly changes of the front should not be read as measurements.
  • Computed here. Areas and distances of the outlines are computed from the pixels, not taken from a source. The reference outline in the viewer is the official inventory outline (SGI 2023).
  • Not settled. The licences of the weights and of the height tiles are still to be read before anything like this is published.

Glacier thickness 2016 (layer)

The layer "Glacier thickness 2016" in the map and 3D menus colours the glacier by its ice thickness. It is the ice thickness map of Grab et al. (2021), "Ice thickness distribution of all Swiss glaciers based on extended ground-penetrating radar data and glaciological modeling", Journal of Glaciology 67(266), read from the ice thickness layer of swisstopo (GLAMOS data).

  • A model, not a measurement. The thickness is modelled from ground-penetrating radar surveys and glaciological modelling. Only the radar points (layer "Radar points") are measurements, on the days and places a survey covers.
  • One state for every year. The map shows the glacier around 2016. The reference year of the map differs by glacier (2017 for Aletsch), so the menu says 2016 as a round name. The ice has thinned since; the layer stays the same when you change the year.
  • Cut to the glacier. The picture is cut to the glacier, so nothing is shown on rock around it.

Radar points (layer)

The layer "Radar points" shows ice thickness measured by ground-penetrating radar. The source is GLAMOS, "Glacier ice thickness from ground-penetrating radar", release 2020 (doi:10.18750/icethickness.2020.r2020, CC BY 4.0). The surveys were made between 1958 and 2017, depending on the glacier.

  • Measured, with a date. These are the only thickness numbers on the site that come from a measurement. The ice has thinned since the survey, so a point is the thickness of the survey year, not of the year you are looking at.
  • One dot is a cell. Each dot is the mean of the radar points inside a 50 m cell (computed here from the points). Where a cell was measured in several years, the dot is marked and a click shows all of them.
  • In 3D. The 3D view shows only about ten pins, a representative one on the middle line of the ice in each stretch of the glacier, to keep loading and moving fast.
  • Against the model. The modelled layer "Glacier thickness 2016" is built on radar surveys like these; where the two differ, the radar points are the measurement.

Ice speed (layer)

The layer "Ice speed" shows how fast the ice surface moves, as a yearly mean. It comes from the ITS_LIVE annual mosaics of image pair velocities, version 2.1 (120 m, one mosaic per RGI region, 2013 to 2024), NASA MEaSUREs ITS_LIVE project, Gardner et al. (its-live.jpl.nasa.gov, CC BY 4.0). The product follows features of the ice surface between pairs of satellite images (Landsat 8/9, Sentinel-2 and Sentinel-1).

  • Copied, not made here. The values are the product's; they are only cut to the picture box of the glacier. The map shows the year of the picture you are looking at, and years without a map show nothing.
  • Slow ice is left clear. Under 5 m/yr the product cannot tell ice from rock (rock beside the glaciers reads 1 to 2 m/yr), so nothing is drawn there. Only the ice inside the oldest outline is shown.
  • A surface speed. It is the speed of the surface, not of the ice deeper down, and a yearly mean says nothing about the season.
  • Resolution. At 120 m the mosaic cannot show narrow ice streams or the last tens of metres of a thin tongue.

Super-resolution layer (3D view)

The optional layer "Super-resolution" in the 3D view shows the Sentinel-2 picture of a year after a super-resolution model, SEN2SR (tacofoundation, model card licence cc0-1.0), has been run over it: 10 m pixels become 2.5 m pixels. It exists for the years 2016 to 2026 of the ten Swiss glaciers of this site that have such a picture; other years show the normal picture.

  • An estimate, not a measurement. The extra detail is the model's guess at what lies between the 10 m pixels. The satellite did not see it. Do not read crevasses, boulders or debris edges off this layer as data.
  • Off by default. Every other picture on the site is the original Sentinel-2 image, never sharpened this way. The layer is only a view; no number on the site is taken from it.
  • Credit. Contains modified Copernicus Sentinel data.
