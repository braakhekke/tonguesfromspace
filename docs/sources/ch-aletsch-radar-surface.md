## Result (corrected after reading the paper)
- The helicopter surveys have a measured surface height by method: DGPS antenna altitude minus the radar-measured air layer, with constant time shifts checked against a DEM "when an up-to-date" one was available. So `z_surface` is not simply a DEM copy, as I had inferred from the 2009 and 2011 values agreeing (median difference +0.2 m, identical in only 3 of 558 spots, standard deviation 3.8 m).
- It is still not a clean 2011 measurement: the DEM check tied the heights to some DEM (not named, no date), the platform positions are "most likely" less accurate than submeter, and the file's `z_surface` median stays the same in 2009 and 2011 although the ice was thinning (thickness -2.4 m). A 2009 and 2011 surface that differ by +0.2 m on average, with 3.8 m scatter, cannot show a thinning of a few metres.
- Surface height of the survey: usable with an uncertainty of several metres (the scatter above, computed), and not as a way to see the change 2009 to 2011.
- Thickness repeatability: the bed implied by the two surveys scatters with a standard deviation of 17.9 m at the same spots.
- Open: which DEM the heights were checked against (ask A. Bauder, VAW).

## Cross-check on ground that is ice free now (computed 2026-10-30, `experiments/aletsch-yearly-dem/radar_icefree_check.py`, local)
Idea (owner): radar points of 2009 and 2011 that lie where the newest swissALTI3D shows ground, not ice. There the exposed ground is the bed, so the radar's bed (`bedrock` column = `z_surface - thickness`) and swissBEDROCK's BEM can be compared with the ground of the newest DEM.
- **Ice-free rule (conservative):** on the tongue mask (plus 300 m), outside the SGI2023 outline (GLAMOS inventory SGI2023, acquisition year 2023, all glaciers) by at least 30 m, and inside the SGI2016 outline (the ice the thickness model of Grab et al. was made for) by at least 30 m, so the model had ice there. Newest swissALTI3D edition per tile (2025 or 2026), DEM slope at the point at most 20 degrees (gentle ground, so a few metres of horizontal error do not matter). No lake or debris mask.
- **Result, 142 points (2009: 81, 2011: 61), ice thickness there median 79 m:** radar bed minus ground now: median +6.6 m (2009) and +8.7 m (2011), middle half +4.3 to +8.5 m (2009) and -1.0 to +19.1 m (2011); 81 % of the points have the radar bed above the exposed ground, median 12 % of the thickness. BEM minus ground now: median +0.5 m (2009) and +2.7 m (2011), middle half -1.9 to +3.1 m and -0.9 to +5.1 m.
- **Sensitivity:** margin 30 m with slope up to 30 degrees (195 points): radar +6.4 m, BEM +0.8 m, the same. Margin 50 m leaves 12 points (all 2009): radar +7.8 m, BEM -2.1 m. A margin of 100 m leaves none (the tongue is narrow).
- **Reading:** at the margins the swissBEDROCK bed sits within a few metres of the exposed ground, and the radar-derived bed lies about 7 m (about a tenth of the thickness) above it, so the radar thickness is too small or the survey surface too high by that much. The sample is margin ice (thickness about 80 m), not the deep tongue; positions at the margin can be off by metres; debris or lake water on the exposed ground is not screened.
