# Data sources and licences

Data sources and licences. The README keeps the summary of the three licences. The first list is Switzerland, the public region; the second list holds the other sources the site names (the same wording as the Sources list in About, `SOURCES` in `js/07-render.js`, copied on 2026-11-09: if they differ, the page is the one to correct, and this file with it).


- **Imagery:** Copernicus Sentinel-2 L1C and L2A via the Copernicus Data Space Ecosystem. Contains modified Copernicus Sentinel data. Free for any use with attribution.
- **Glacier areas and outlines:** GLAMOS Swiss Glacier Inventory, editions SGI1850, SGI1973, SGI2016 and SGI2023 (areas, outlines, ranking by area), CC BY 4.0.
- **Length change:** GLAMOS (2025), Swiss Glacier Length Change, release 2025, doi:10.18750/lengthchange.2025.r2025. Free for scientific and **non-commercial** use with the source indicated. The length today is read from the GLAMOS figure [lc_stat_frequency](https://doi.glamos.ch/figures/lc_stat_frequency.pdf).
- **Volume change:** GLAMOS (2025), Swiss Glacier Volume Change, release 2025, doi:10.18750/volumechange.2025.r2025. Geodetic volume change of each glacier from elevation models; the file says: free for scientific and **non-commercial** use with the source indicated (the landing page says CC BY 4.0).
- **Yearly mass balance:** GLAMOS (2025), Swiss Glacier Mass Balance, release 2025, doi:10.18750/massbalance.2025.r2025. Direct glacier-wide balance of about 40 glaciers (four of the ten: Aletsch, Rhone, Corbassière, Findel); the file says non-commercial use.
- **Swiss-wide volume:** GLAMOS (2025), Swisswide Glacier Mass Balance, release 2025, doi:10.18750/massbalance.swisswide.2025.r2025. Yearly volume of all Swiss glaciers; the same non-commercial wording in the file.
- **Radar ice thickness:** GLAMOS (2020), Swiss Glacier Ice Thickness, release 2020, doi:10.18750/icethickness.2020.r2020 (CC BY 4.0). Ground-penetrating radar points, thinned to one mean per 50 m cell.
- **Bed under the ice (3D thickness cross-section):** swissBEDROCK, Federal Office of Topography swisstopo, release 1 (August 2025), band BEM, 10 m, https://www.swisstopo.admin.ch/en/3d-swissbedrock-en. Free geodata: use, processing and commercial use allowed, credit mandatory (https://www.swisstopo.admin.ch/en/terms-of-use-free-geodata-and-geoservices, version 1.3.2021). Under glaciers it is swissALTI3D 10 m minus a modelled ice thickness (GLAMOS 2020), "a maximum estimate of the bedrock surface" (release notes). Licence ledger: `docs/sources/ch-swissbedrock.md`. Baked into `data/terrain/<id>.bin` by `scripts/build_terrain_bundle.py` (the header of each of the ten Swiss files names it: `bed: swissBEDROCK, band BEM`).
- **Street View panoramas (Aletsch):** © Google, photographed by Google or by visitors (the contributor is named in the panel). They are shown in Google's own viewer (Maps Embed API, free); only the panorama ids are stored (`data/streetview.json`), which Google allows. Without the key `STREETVIEW_EMBED_KEY` (see below) the layer is not offered.
- **Yearly volume loss:** GLAMOS and the Swiss Academy of Sciences (SCNAT) annual glacier reports.
- **Ice thickness and volume per glacier:** Grab et al. (2021), Journal of Glaciology 67(266), via the swisstopo map service. **Thickest ice per glacier (number, year of the map, place of the cell):** the data collection SwissGlacierThickness-R2020 (ETH Research Collection, doi:10.3929/ethz-b-000434697, CC BY 4.0): `h_max` and `year_mode` of `Summary.shp`, place from the glacier's `IceThickness.tif`; built by `build_outlines.py --model-only` into `glaciers.js` (`model`). Ledger: docs/sources/switzerland-grab-thickness.md.
- **Ice volume as a block, the size page in 3D (`data/ice-volumes.json`, `scripts/build_ice_volumes.py`, `size.html`):** per glacier `V_2016` ("Ice volume (km3) homogenised for the year 2016"), `V_year` and `year_mode` from `01_Summary/Summary.shp` of Grab & Melchior (2020), SwissGlacierThickness-R2020, doi:10.3929/ethz-b-000434697, **CC BY 4.0**, copied as stored (the raster integrals were checked against `V_year`: equal to four digits for all ten). Mean loss: `dV_km3` between the survey years `from` and `to` of GLAMOS Swiss Glacier Volume Change (**non-commercial**), divided here by `to - from` (computed, labelled so, held constant in the melt). Place of a block on its own glacier: `at` in `data/ice-volumes.json` is the middle of the 2023 outline (the point of its biggest piece farthest from the edge, computed by `scripts/build_ice_volumes.py`); the page then looks for the flattest ground inside the outline, away from its edge (`glacierSpot`), so that the block stays on its own ice. 3D terrain: Mapterhorn tiles (https://mapterhorn.com/attribution, Terrarium webp, 512 px, zoom 11 to 13), picture EOX Sentinel-2 cloudless 2024 (CC BY-NC-SA 4.0). The 3D library (three.js 0.147.0, MIT) is loaded from cdn.jsdelivr.net only when 3D is switched on. Friction (0.45), bounce (0.15) and gravity (60 times the true one, so that blocks of this size fall like dice) are assumed values, not measurements, and the page says so.
- **Tall buildings on the size page (`data/tall-buildings.json`, `scripts/build_tall_buildings.py`, `size.html` switch "Tall buildings", off at first, desktop only):** swissBUILDINGS3D 3.0 Beta, © Federal Office of Topography swisstopo (STAC collection `ch.swisstopo.swissbuildings3d_3_0`, file geodatabase tiles, LV95), swisstopo free geodata: swisstopo's terms page (read 2026-11-08) says the data "may be enriched and processed and also used commercially" and "A reference to the source is mandatory" ("©swisstopo" or "Federal Office of Topography swisstopo"). Layer `Building_solid`, newest edition of the tiles under the nine cities. **Height:** where the tile states `GESAMTHOEHE` (buildings with an EGID: all of Bern and St. Gallen, most of Zürich, Basel, Lucerne) it is copied and the limit is over 25 m (our limit); the other tiles (Geneva, Lausanne, Lugano, Zermatt and some Zürich buildings) state none, so there the height is **computed** here as the highest minus the lowest point of the 3D solid (field `how: computed`; in the one Zürich tile where both exist the computed height is about 3.4 m more than the stated one, median, so a few buildings of about 22 to 25 m may be included). `DACH_MAX` is not used: in the Lausanne tiles it lies about 48 m above the top of the solid. A computed height over 200 m is dropped as a geometry error (one in Geneva). Footprint (faces of the solid projected and merged, simplified to 0.5 m) and placement on the page's Mapterhorn terrain are computed too. The source page says the product is a Beta and that some tiles carry old time stamps. The buildings are solid for the ice blocks (collision points on walls and roofs, `PB` in `size.html`).
- **Water use:** SVGW water statistics.
- **Background:** Sentinel-2 cloudless 2024 by EOX IT Services GmbH ([s2maps.eu](https://s2maps.eu)), CC BY-NC-SA 4.0. **Base map and place labels:** © OpenStreetMap contributors.
- **Font:** Schibsted Grotesk, SIL Open Font Licence 1.1 (`fonts/OFL.txt`).
- **Yearly ice surfaces of the ten Swiss glaciers (3D view, 2016 to 2026; `data/yearly/<id>.json`, `provenance.html`):** as the page's Sources list states it: Yearly ice surfaces of the ten Swiss glaciers of this site (3D view, 2016 to 2026): swissALTI3D (2019, 2024 and 2025 editions), swissSURFACE3D raster and the Spezialbefliegungen flight DSMs, © [Federal Office of Topography swisstopo](https://www.swisstopo.admin.ch) (open government data, source reference mandatory; terms read on 2026-11-06); Grab et al. 2021 ice thickness (CC BY 4.0, doi:10.3929/ethz-b-000434697); MeteoSwiss daily temperature; GLAMOS mass balance per elevation band (release 2025, [doi:10.18750/massbalance.2025.r2025](https://doi.org/10.18750/massbalance.2025.r2025), non-commercial use); ICESat-2 ATL06 laser heights, for checking only. A model, not a measurement, except on the days and cells a survey covers (which years are measured for each glacier is listed under it above and in its field notes). The full account: [Aletsch](../provenance.html?id=B36-26), [Gornergletscher](../provenance.html?id=B56-07), [Fieschergletscher](../provenance.html?id=B40-07), [Rhonegletscher](../provenance.html?id=B43-03), [Unteraargletscher](../provenance.html?id=A54g-11), [Corbassière](../provenance.html?id=B83-03), [Zmuttgletscher](../provenance.html?id=B57-05), [Findelgletscher](../provenance.html?id=B56-03), [Feegletscher](../provenance.html?id=B53-04), [Triftgletscher](../provenance.html?id=A54e-24). swisstopo terms page read 2026-10-30 ("may be used, distributed and made accessible; enriched and processed and also used commercially. A reference to the source is mandatory"); the terms of the special flights were read 2026-11-06 (swisstopo's terms of use, "A reference to the source is mandatory."; ledger `docs/sources/switzerland-spezialbefliegungen.md`). swissBEDROCK (swisstopo) is the floor (`docs/sources/ch-swissbedrock.md`). The airborne DEMs of 2009, 2011 and 2017 of the RAGMAC set are NOT used (readmes: internal use only). **Super-resolved picture (optional 3D layer):** Super-resolved picture (optional 3D layer, the ten Swiss glaciers of this site): SEN2SR (tacofoundation, model card licence cc0-1.0) applied to Sentinel-2 scenes (contains modified Copernicus Sentinel data). The extra detail is the model's estimate. Model card read 2026-10-30.

## Glacier totals on the front page (home.html, under the map)

Read 2026-11-03; each is the count the source states, and what counts as a glacier differs between inventories.

- Switzerland: 1,289 glaciers in SGI2023, counted in the GLAMOS inventory file (inventory_sgi2023_r2026; `inventory.count` in data/glaciers.js). Earlier text used "1,400 individual glacier entities" (SGI2016), Linsbauer et al. 2021, doi 10.3389/feart.2021.704189.
- Iceland 568 and Svalbard 1615 (Svalbard and Jan Mayen): Randolph Glacier Inventory 6.0, regional table at https://rgidata.org/user_guide/regions/overview.html.
- Norway: "3143 glaciers" of the 1999-2006 inventory, Andreassen et al. 2022, Journal of Glaciology 68(272), 1085-1106, read at https://www.cambridge.org/core/product/EEBB73BA4DB7D3E77B25172F66ADBA9F/core-reader. The count of its newer 2018-19 inventory is in Table 3 (an image), NOT READ, so it is not used.

## Other sources named on the site

Copied from the Sources list of the site (see the top of this file). The Icelandic, Norwegian and Svalbard entries belong to regions that are on the public site only when the repository variables `PUBLISH_ICELAND` or `PUBLISH_NORWAY` are `1`. The entry key is in bold (`SOURCES` in `js/07-render.js`).

- **itslive**: ITS_LIVE: annual mosaics of image pair velocities, version 2.1 (120 m, one mosaic per RGI region, 2013 to 2024), NASA MEaSUREs ITS_LIVE project, Gardner et al. [its-live.jpl.nasa.gov](https://its-live.jpl.nasa.gov). CC BY 4.0. Surface speed of the ice, copied from the product; the maps are made from image pairs of Landsat 8/9, Sentinel-2 and Sentinel-1.
- **landsat**: Landsat 5 and 7, Collection 2 Level-2 surface reflectance (30 m), courtesy of the [U.S. Geological Survey](https://www.usgs.gov/landsat-missions) (public domain), read through the [Microsoft Planetary Computer](https://planetarycomputer.microsoft.com/dataset/landsat-c2-l2). One scene per year before 2016, chosen by the Sentinel-2 rules of the region (clear, closest to its target day: the end of the melt season); natural colour and the two false colour styles, made from the red, green, blue, near infrared and two shortwave infrared bands; stripes of no data in Landsat 7 are filled along the ice flow (own processing).
- **terrain**: swissALTI3D © [swisstopo](https://www.swisstopo.admin.ch) (open government data), served as terrain tiles by [Mapterhorn](https://mapterhorn.com/attribution). Peak names: swissNAMES3D © swisstopo.
- **mapterhorn**: Terrain tiles by [Mapterhorn](https://mapterhorn.com/attribution), made from open elevation data: ÍslandsDEM v1.0 (10 m; Náttúrufræðistofnun Íslands, CC BY 4.0) over Iceland, the terrain models (DTM, 1 m) of Kartverket (CC BY 4.0) over Norway, and the S0 terrain model of Svalbard (20 m; Norwegian Polar Institute, CC BY 4.0) over Svalbard. The elevation model is older than the pictures.
- **routes**: SchweizMobil route data of the Federal Roads Office (ASTRA), via [swisstopo geo.admin.ch](https://map.geo.admin.ch); hiking trails: swissTLM3D © swisstopo.
- **photos**: Historic photographs in the 3D view (eight glaciers: Aletsch, Gorner, Fiescher, Unteraar, Zmutt, Rhone, Findel, Fee): ETH Library, Bildarchiv ([e-pics](https://ba.e-pics.ethz.ch)), CC BY-SA 4.0 (each photo shows its own licence), the place and angles of the camera by volunteers on [Smapshot](https://smapshot.heig-vd.ch) (HEIG-VD).
- **names**: Glacier names on the map (layer "Other glacier names"): Wikidata and the Randolph Glacier Inventory.
- **is_rgi**: Randolph Glacier Inventory 6.0 (RGI Consortium 2017), region 06 Iceland, via GLIMS: the drainage basin of the outlet, its length and heights, outlines from about 2000. [doi:10.7265/N5-RGI-60](https://doi.org/10.7265/N5-RGI-60). Terms to be checked for anything beyond non-commercial use.
- **is_imo**: Icelandic Met Office (Veðurstofa Íslands): glacier outlines of Iceland, about 1890 (the Little Ice Age maximum) and 17 September 2025 (Sentinel-2), CC BY 4.0. Hannesdóttir, H. et al. (2020), A national glacier inventory and variations in glacier extent in Iceland from the Little Ice Age maximum to 2019, [Jökull 70, 1–34](https://www.vedur.is/gogn/joklar/J70-1-34-minniupplausn.pdf). Data: [islenskirjoklar.is](https://islenskirjoklar.is) and the WFS of the Icelandic Met Office. The 2025 area is the ice inside the RGI basin, counted on a 100 m grid here.
- **is_gudm**: Guðmundsson, S., Björnsson, H. and Pálsson, F. (2017), Changes of Breiðamerkurjökull glacier, SE-Iceland, from its late nineteenth century maximum to the present, Geografiska Annaler: Series A 99(4), 338–352, [doi:10.1080/04353676.2017.1355216](https://doi.org/10.1080/04353676.2017.1355216): retreat, exposed land, mass loss and mass balance since about 1890.
- **is_arctic**: ArcticDEM strips (2 m), © Polar Geospatial Center, University of Minnesota, CC BY 4.0, made from Maxar WorldView stereo images; found through the [PGC STAC catalogue](https://stac.pgc.umn.edu/api/v1), [pgc.umn.edu/data/arcticdem](https://www.pgc.umn.edu/data/arcticdem/). Used for the 3D view: the surface of each year (the dated strips are listed in About, The 3D view).
- **is_dem**: ÍslandsDEM v1.0 (10 m, composite of ArcticDEM and lidar), © Náttúrufræðistofnun Íslands and Landmælingar Íslands, CC BY 4.0: the ground the strips are fitted to. [ftp.natt.is](https://ftp.natt.is/gisdata/raster/), catalogue [gatt.lmi.is](https://gatt.lmi.is).
- **is_osm**: OpenStreetMap contributors (ODbL): footpaths and tracks, peak and place names, via the [Overpass API](https://overpass-api.de). [openstreetmap.org/copyright](https://www.openstreetmap.org/copyright).
- **is_wiki**: Wikipedia, [Breiðamerkurjökull](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brei%C3%B0amerkurj%C3%B6kull) and [Jökulsárlón](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J%C3%B6kuls%C3%A1rl%C3%B3n) (CC BY-SA 4.0): the name, the lagoon, the history and the news item, with the sources named there (Iceland on the web, Guðmundsson et al., BBC, Vísir, RÚV).
- **no_nve**: NVE, the Norwegian Water Resources and Energy Directorate: glacier inventory of mainland Norway 2018–2019 (outlines made from Sentinel-2 images, areas, lengths and heights of the glacier units; a glacier is the units that hang together). Andreassen, L. M. (2022), Breer og fonner i Norge, NVE Rapport 3-2022; Andreassen, L. M. et al. (2022), An inventory of Norway's glaciers and ice-marginal lakes from 2018–19 Sentinel-2 data, Journal of Glaciology 68(272), 1085–1106, [doi:10.1017/jog.2022.20](https://doi.org/10.1017/jog.2022.20). Data: the map service Bre of NVE ([nve.no/hydrology/glaciers](https://www.nve.no/hydrology/glaciers/)); NVE asks to cite these two publications. Terms to be checked for anything beyond non-commercial use. The area of Norway, 2,328 km², and the list of the ten largest glaciers are from NVE's glacier page.
- **no_old**: NVE, glacier inventory of 1947–1985 (outlines from topographic maps and aerial photographs; the year of the outline differs by glacier), the layer Breflate_Historisk of the same map service. Used for the older outline and area.
- **sj_npi**: Norwegian Polar Institute: glacier outlines of Svalbard, 1936–1972 (aerial photographs, mainly 1936, 1966 and 1971), 1990, 2001–2010 (SPOT-5, Landsat and ASTER images) and 2020 (Sentinel-2 mosaic): one polygon per glacier unit with the same ident in every epoch, the map service Isbreer – overflatetyper og utstrekning of [geodata.npolar.no](https://geodata.npolar.no). Length and heights are of the 2001–2010 outlines. Terms to be checked for anything beyond non-commercial use.
