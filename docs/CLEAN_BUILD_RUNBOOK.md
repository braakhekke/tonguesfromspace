# Runbook: the clean elevation build (surfaces at the Sentinel-2 dates) for any Swiss glacier

Written 2026-11-03 from the one run that exists, Grosser Aletsch (B36-26): `experiments/aletsch-clean/`, report `docs/sources/switzerland-aletsch-clean-build.md`, viewer `experiments/aletsch-clean/compare_aletsch_clean.html`. **Nothing of this is on the site.** The site still shows what `docs/YEARLY_GLACIER_RUNBOOK.md` produces; this file describes the replacement the owner asked to test. Where this file says "Aletsch did X", that is a measured fact of that run (numbers in the report); where it says "set by the rule", the rule is what to apply to the next glacier.

## 0. Status of the code (read first)
**Updated 2026-11-03 (evening).** The package exists: `experiments/clean-yearly/` (`params.py`, `common.py`, `model.py`, `s01_ground` … `s10_viewer`, `run.sh`; `GLACIER=<SGI id> THICKNESS_TIF=<IceThickness.tif> PY=<venv python> ./run.sh`). Run for Aletsch it reproduces the single-glacier run (`experiments/aletsch-clean/`, which stays as the first record): same ice and volume per date, same lake levels. **Aletsch is on the site (local working tree, not committed)**; the nine others are not (state in the table of section 14). `s09_site_files.py` writes the two files the site reads, `data/yearly/<id>.npz` (surfaces, int16 decimetres above `base`, on the 14 m nodes of the terrain file; `clean` flag) and `data/yearly/<id>.json` (field notes: `clean` block with chart data, surveys table, texts, map with flight footprints); `s12_textures.py` (runs before s09/s10 in `run.sh`) fetches the Sentinel-2 pictures of the strip's bounding box (one Copernicus Process API request per year, 10 m, the site's colours) into `experiments/clean-yearly/<id>/s2_textures/`; `s10_viewer.py` writes `data/yearly/<id>-viewer.html`, which draws exactly the 3D strip (quads wholly outside it are left out). Run s09 **before** s10. Then `python3 scripts/build_terrain_bundle.py --id <id>` (its `clean` branch takes the surfaces as they are: no taper, no resampling) and `python3 scripts/board_sync.py`.

## 1. Rules that stay (CLAUDE.md, owner)

## 3. Grid
8 m, axis-aligned LV95, origin at a multiple of 1000 m (so 125 x 125 cells per 1 km tile; 4 x 4 per cell of the 2 m swissALTI3D; 16 x 16 of the 0.5 m DSM). Extent: bounding box of the four corner nodes of the site's terrain file, minus 100 m and plus 100 m, rounded outward to whole kilometres; `strip` = cells inside the corner polygon buffered by 40 m (the strip edge in LV95 axes is off by the meridian convergence, up to 90 m at the far ends). Rows run south to north. Do not use another resolution without re-deriving: finer than 8 m is not supported by the 10 m bed and 4 m DSM overview; the site's display grid is the 14 m nodes of its terrain file (0.55 M nodes for Aletsch; 8 m would be 3 times that; the detail lost at 14 m on the tongue is 1.0 m rms).

## 4. Steps (script, command, what it does, gate)
Run from the package folder with `GLACIER=<id>`; after each step read the printed lines against the gate before the next.

**s01 ground.** `python s01_ground.py` → `cache/ground.npz` (`A1`, `A2`, `ed1`, `ed2`). Each 8 m cell = mean of the 2 m points of the tile (header line `X Y Z` is read with `header=0`). Gate: no missing tile, NaN share 0 % inside the strip. Record the edition year of every tile (Aletsch: all 2024; the southern Bernese glaciers have 2025).

**s02 flights.** `python s02_flights.py` → `cache/flights.npz` (one 8 m array per flight), `flights.json`. STAC query by the grid bbox (EPSG:4326); every item with a DSM-RASTER asset; read the 0.5 m cloud-optimised file at its 4 m overview (nearest at that level), pixels outside 500 to 5000 m or equal to nodata are NaN, 2 x 2 mean to 8 m, a cell needs 3 valid of 4; keep a flight with more than 5000 valid cells. **Rock tie:** stable rock = cells where `|A2 - A1| < 1.5 m`, slope (of A2) < 30 deg, 1500 to 3600 m, farther than 20 cells (160 m) from any cell with `|A2 - A1| > 3 m`, inside the strip. Fit `DSM - A2 = a + b (E - 2646000)/1000 + c (N - 1142000)/1000` (the origin is the Aletsch centre: use the glacier's own), 6 iterations of least squares with a 2.5 x NMAD clip; **plane if the flight has at least 5000 stable cells, else the median offset only; skip a flight with fewer than 1500.** Gate: NMAD of `DSM - A2` on stable rock after the tie at most 1.1 m per flight (Aletsch: 0.55 to 1.04 m; offsets up to 1.7 m and tilts up to 0.45 m/km were removed). A flight failing the gate is dropped and named.

**s03 lidar.** `python s03_lidar.py` → `cache/lidar.npz` (`dsm`, `day` = ordinal), `lidar.json`. For every tile in `lidar_dates.json`: date = the day with at least 85 % of the points; else, if every day with more than 2 % of the points lies within 60 days, the point-weighted mean day (campaign tile, sigma 0.5 m instead of 0.35 m); else the tile is not used. Read `swisssurface3d-raster_<year>_<E>-<N>_0.5_2056_5728.tif` at 125 x 125 cells (overview average, masked), years tried in order (Aletsch 2021, 2022; other glaciers: the years `lidar_dates.json` shows). One median offset to `A2` on stable rock. Gate: offset below 0.1 m and NMAD below 0.1 m (swissALTI3D is made from this lidar; Aletsch: 0.00 and 0.02 m). If the offset is larger the tile year or the stable mask is wrong.

**s04 forcing.** `python s04_forcing.py` → `cache/smb.npz` (`zs` = 1500 to 4100 m step 50, `ords`, `H[z, day]`). Column model per 50 m elevation, daily from 1 Oct 2015: winter (1 Oct to 30 Apr) accumulation `Bw` evenly (a negative `Bw` is melt, evenly); summer (1 May to 30 Sep) melt `-Bs` spread in proportion to `max(T(z,d) - T0, 0)`, `T(z,d) = T_station(d) + lapse (z - z_station)`; melt takes snow first (height = we / 0.40), then ice (`/0.90`, bin with `Ba < 0`) or firn (`/0.60`, bin with `Ba >= 0`); snow left on 30 Sep becomes firn (`snow (1/0.60 - 1/0.40)`). Defaults T0 = 0 C, lapse -0.0065 K/m, snow 0.40. Temperature: station record gap-filled by regression on the second station (common days since 2010). Years 2016 to 2026 (the 2026 year runs to the end of the temperature record). Gate: net lowering since 1 Oct 2015 at the tongue of the order of 10 m per year for Aletsch (bins carry 8 to 13 m w.e.); a figure near zero means the water-equivalent to height conversion was left out (densities are relative to water).

**s05 mask.** `python s05_mask.py` → `cache/mask.npz` (`gl`, `o23`, `o16`). `gl` = SGI2016 outline + SGI2023 outline + connected ground within 600 m of them that dropped more than 4 m between A1 and A2, opened once and closed twice, inside the strip. Polygons are repaired with `buffer(0)` (the SGI file has invalid rings). Gate: `gl` area within 5 % of the SGI2016/SGI2023 areas inside the strip plus the dropped cells (Aletsch: 18.47, 17.40, 19.71 km2).

**s05a timing.** `python s05a_timing.py` → `cache/timing.json`. Compare the modelled surface change between two dates of one summer with the DSM difference on the ablation zone (pairs: same-summer DSM pairs and the DSM of one date against the lidar tiles of the same summer; bins of 100 m with more than 200 cells; the pairs of Aletsch are written in the script and must be rebuilt from `flights.json`/`lidar.json` for another glacier). Grid of T0 {-2,-1,0,1,2,3} x lapse {-0.0050,-0.0060,-0.0069} x snow density {0.35,0.40,0.50}. **Rule: take the grid's best only if its rmse is at least 15 % lower than the defaults' rmse; else keep the physical defaults** (Aletsch: 1.16 vs 1.12 m, defaults kept). Report the pairs that fail by more than 1 m (Aletsch: the two September pairs of 2018 and 2020, where the model melts 1 to 2 m too much).

**s05b fit.** `python s05b_fit.py` → `cache/fit.npz`, `fit.json`. Per glacier cell: `h = h0 + S(z0, t) + e tau + w r`. Observations: every DSM flight (sigma 0.7 m; footprint eroded by 2 cells; for cells outside the 2023 outline (dilated one cell) only if `DSM - A2 > 2.5 m`, i.e. ice), the lidar (0.35 m or 0.5 m), A1 and A2 as ice surfaces (stereo sigma 0.6 m below 2000 m, 1.5 m above, plus the date uncertainty 20 d (A1) or 15 d (A2) times the local melt rate; A2 only inside the dilated 2023 outline). `z0` = mean of A1 and A2 on the 2023 ice, `A1 - 8 m` elsewhere, clipped to 1500 to 4100 m. `tau` = years since 2020-01-01. Order, all fixed:
1. Weighted least squares per cell for (h0, e) from DSM and lidar only with a very weak prior (e = 0, sigma 6 m/yr); the **tongue** = cells with at least 3 DSM/lidar dates spanning more than 730 days inside the 2023 outline.
2. **Dates of the editions are estimated:** with the DSM+lidar fit (and for A1 also A2 included), the date at which the median residual of the edition over the tongue crosses zero (scan 2022-06 to 2024-06 for A2, 2017-03 to 2017-12 for A1). Aletsch: A2 2023-09-06 (swisstopo names strips of 6 Sep 2023: agreement), A1 2017-09-01 after the balance correction (before it the crossing was not found). Gate: A1/A2 dates within the survey season; if no crossing, use the documented date and say so.
3. **Summer-balance factor per hydrological year** `kappa_y` (multiplies `Bs` of that year, all elevations): coordinate descent over the hydrological years from the first to the last year that a DSM or lidar epoch touches (Aletsch 2017 to 2025), three sweeps (candidates +-0.2, +-0.1 first, then +-0.05), range 0.5 to 1.6, objective = mean over all observations of `min((r/sigma)^2, 16)` on every third tongue cell. Years without a DSM keep 1.00 (Aletsch 2016, 2026). A factor that did not move from 1.00 is reported as "no improvement", not as measured. Result for Aletsch: 2017 0.90, 2018 0.80, 2019 0.80, 2020 1.00, 2021 0.85, 2022 1.05, 2023 1.00, 2024 1.00, 2025 1.05.
4. **Edition bias:** residual of each edition against the fit on cells with lidar or at least 3 DSM dates, median per 100 m of `z0` (1700 to 3100), bins with more than 2000 cells only; piecewise linear between bin centres, constant above the highest bin, zero below the lowest; subtracted from the edition; 0.5 m added in quadrature to the edition sigma above 2400 m (this 2400 m is Aletsch's lidar reach: use the highest bin centre of the glacier). Aletsch: A2 +1.0 m at 2250, +1.8 at 2350, +1.5 at 2450; A1 +0.2, +0.7, +0.6.
5. Pass 1: (h0, e) from all observations (prior sigma 6). Pass 2: prior = the first-pass `e` smoothed with a Gaussian of 31 cells (250 m) over the glacier mask, sigma 1.0 m/yr.
6. Residual of every observation against the final fit is saved with its day and sigma.
Gates: residual NMAD over all observations at most 1.3 m (Aletsch 1.00 m, rmse 1.88 m); median `e` by 200 m of elevation printed and read: it must be positive on the tongue and fall to zero or below in the accumulation area (Aletsch +2.4 to +3.1 m/yr below 2300 m, -0.2 to -1.0 m/yr above 2700 m). A tongue with negative `e` means the balance is too small or the editions are mis-dated.

**s05c bed.** `python s05c_bed.py <path to IceThickness.tif>` → `cache/bed.npz`. Thickness bilinear 10 m to 8 m (values below 0.5 m set to 0); `bed = A1 - thickness` where the map has ice inside `gl`; elsewhere in `gl` the lower of A1 and A2; outside `gl` the ground is A2. Then **lower the bed to `z - 0.5 m`** wherever a DSM or lidar surface that stands more than 2.5 m above the ground lies below the bed. Gate: lowered cells at most 2 % of the thickness-map ice (Aletsch 0.7 %, 0.134 km2). Never use a hard floor on a measured surface (Gorner: the DSM lies below swissBEDROCK on 3 to 8 % of the ice cells; 96 to 100 % of those are inside the thickness uncertainty of Grab et al.).

**s06 surfaces.** `python s06_surfaces.py` → `cache/surfaces.npz`, `surfaces.json`. For every picture date t: `h = h0 + S(z0,t) + e tau(t) + w r(t)` with `w = 1.9^2/(1.9^2 + sigma_k^2)` per observation (so a DSM epoch is reproduced to about 0.2 m), residual interpolated linearly in time between epochs and decaying with `exp(-dt/0.75 yr)` before the first and after the last epoch. `surface = max(h, bed)` inside `gl`, ground elsewhere; ice where `surface - bed > 0.5 m`. **Margin:** outside `gl`, cells within 32 m (4 cells) of an ice cell whose ice surface stands more than 0.5 m above the ground there and whose own ground is not lower than the nearest ice cell's ground minus 1 m take the nearest ice surface (flat continuation up a wall; Aletsch 0.63 km2 in 2016, 0.10 km2 from 2023). **Lakes: section 6.** Gate: for a date on which a flight was flown, the surface minus that flight on its cells has rmse at most 0.3 m (Aletsch 0.24 m).

**s07 validate.** `python s07_validate.py` → `cache/validation.json`. V1 ICESat-2 (shots on ice, model minus shot on the shot's day, by year and by elevation); V2 leave one flight (and the lidar) out, refit (`model.py`: `two_pass`), predict that flight's day on its cells, with and without the residual; V3 GEDI (one rock offset from footprints off the ice); V4 stakes (annual files from the year after A1; stake minus bin balance and minus the kappa-scaled balance). Report all numbers; there is no pass/fail except: (a) a bias of the model above the lidar reach against ICESat-2 that exceeds the bias where lidar exists by more than 1 m means the edition bias (step 4) is not removed enough; (b) V2 rmse above 3 m for a flight inside the DSM years means the fit is wrong, not the DSM. Aletsch: ICESat-2 +0.76 m median, NMAD 1.43, rmse 2.42 (cells with lidar +0.89, without +2.70 before the bias removal), V2 0.9 to 2.8 m for 2017 to 2023, 4.4 and 4.8 m for 2024 and 2025 (extrapolation, collapsing tip).

**s08 export and viewer.** `python s08_export.py` → `aletsch_clean_8m.npz` (8 m, all dates: `z`, `thickness`, `ice`, `cls`, `bed`, `ground`) and `node_<name>_clean.npz` (the 14 m nodes of the site's terrain file, bilinear; `cls`: 0 not ice, 1 editions + balance + emergence only, 2 DSM/lidar of another year, 3 DSM/lidar of that year, 4 margin continuation, 5 lake); `python build_viewer.py` → the comparer. The node file needs `E, N, H, bed, site, crop, dx, dz` from the site's terrain file and the texture extent; Aletsch's came from `experiments/gorner-new-dems/node_aletsch.npz` (made by `new_node.py`, which needs the OLD method's `new_<name>.npz`): replace this dependency by a function in the package that builds the node file from `site_grid.load(<id>)` and `meta.json`. Gate: left panel (decoded site file) and right panel are drawn on identical nodes; screenshot every year with `mode=prov`.

## 5. Mass-balance forcing for glaciers without their own series
Aletsch, Rhone, Corbassière and Findel have GLAMOS bins of their own. For every other glacier use the donor mechanism of `experiments/swiss-yearly-dem/shape.py` (`use_donor(<id>)`, or `use_line()`, the straight line fitted to Findel, Adler, Aletsch, Rhone and Corbassière) exactly as `params.json` of that glacier states it, evaluated at the glacier's elevations. Mark the surface as "borrowed balance" in the notes, and set `kappa_y = 1.00` unless the glacier has at least 4 DSM years on at least 3 km2 of ice. The ICESat-2 check decides whether the borrowed balance is acceptable (bias against ICESat-2 within 1 m of the bias where lidar exists).

## 6. Lakes (owner instruction 2026-11-03: every lake flattened today is flat in the new models)
Three groups exist today; all three must be flat water surfaces in the **stored** new surface of every date, so that no consumer sees a sloping lake.

**A. Lakes flattened inside the stored model (today only Aletsch's tip lake, 2024 to 2026).** Done in `s06_surfaces.py` (block for `B36-26` only; the mask is `experiments/aletsch-yearly-dem/lake_masks_coast.npz`, 8 m cell in the lake when its centre is within 5 m of a mask pixel). Level estimator `lake_level`: the fullest 0.2 m bin of the DSM cells over the mask, then the median of the cells within 1.0 m of it; quality `plateau` when at least half the cells lie within 0.3 m of the level, else `weak` (wavy water or shore in the mask, level +-0.5 m); a year without a flight holds the previous level (`held`, +-1 m). The mask is closed once and a one-cell dike ring lower than level - 1 m is raised to the level (the old model's rim rule, now applied); the bundle builder keeps ground above the water (`LAKE_WALL`). Aletsch result: 2024 1642.11 m (plateau, 62 % within 0.3 m), 2025 1642.76 m (weak, 27 %, +-0.5 m), 2026 1642.76 m (held, +-1 m). The older assumed levels (1641.4, 1639.5, 1632.7 m) are not used. For another glacier with a lake mask, copy the block into a generic function with the mask as a parameter; do not hard-code years.

**B. Lakes the 3D view flattens at run time (`LAKE_GLACIERS` in `js/11-view3d.js`: Rhone `rhone`, Findel `sgi-B56-03`, Zmutt `zmutt`).** The browser finds the water of each year by colour in the natural-colour picture (`isWaterRGB`: mean of r,g,b between 70 and 200, `g - r >= 15`, `b - r >= 12`, `|b - g| <= 12`; a vertex is water when most of its 3 x 3 pixels are) and sets every water component (at least 20 vertices, at least 3 vertices with water all round) to the level of the **plateau of the newest swissALTI3D edition inside it** (bins of 0.4 m, the fullest bin and its neighbours must hold at least `max(20, one third of the component)` vertices; level = mean within 1.5 bins; vertices within 40 m of the level set to it; then four rings of lower pockets, up to 25 m deep, filled). Consequences for the new product: (1) the base terrain (the newest edition `H`) must stay exactly as it is, because the plateau is read from it; (2) inside every water component outside the ice the new surface must equal `H` (the clean build uses `A2` outside `gl`, which satisfies this; do not let the margin continuation or the bed lowering touch these cells: exclude the water mask of that date from step 6's wedge); (3) the clean build must **also** flatten them in the stored surface: port `flattenLakes` (parameters above) to Python, run it per date on the 14 m node surface with the water mask of that date's picture, and keep the runtime function as a safety net. **Test (add to `tests/`):** for each of Rhone, Findel and Zmutt and each year, every vertex of every water component of the new stored surface has `|h - level| <= 0.05 m`, and running the browser's `flattenLakes` on the stored surface returns the same array (idempotent). Never change `js/11-view3d.js` for this; compare screenshots before and after at the lake for several years (old runbook rule).

**C. Radar and bed near lakes.** Radar "ground now" points on a flat water surface (flat to 15 cm within 8 m, or below level + 3 m) are left out of any bed check (`bed_check.py`); bed and thickness under a lake are not defined: the lake mask is a separate class (`cls` 5), `thickness` is 0 there.

## 10. What a future site integration needs (not done, owner decision)
The terrain bundle builder `scripts/build_terrain_bundle.py` today reads `data/yearly/<id>.npz` (change against the newest edition, 20 m, cm) and resamples it with `taper` and the bed floor. For the clean build it would instead take the 14 m surfaces of `node_<name>_clean.npz` directly (no taper, no resampling), keep the array order (heights, yearly surfaces, bed last), keep the 28 m coarse grid rule (nA, nB even; nB a multiple of 4), carry the bed of the clean build, and write the surface date of each year (the picture date, not `day: '09-30'`) into the header (`yearly.day`). `js/11-view3d.js` writes "on 30 September" in the terrain note (line about `terrainNote`) and `provenance.html` reads `data/yearly/<id>.json`: both texts must change with it. The field notes get the remarks of the report (flux closure not done; the high accumulation area good to about 2 to 3 m absolute).

## 11. Reduced-data modes
- **At least 3 DSM/lidar dates over more than two years on some ice (a "tongue" exists):** the full procedure.
- **One or two DSM/lidar dates:** no tongue, so steps 2 to 4 of s05b cannot be estimated. Use the documented dates of the editions (A1 survey year and A2 flight dates from the swissALTI3D Ausgabebericht and the strip layer), `kappa_y = 1.00`, no edition bias correction but **edition sigma 2.0 m above 2000 m** and an explicit statement; keep the DSM/lidar epochs as observations (they still fix the surface on their day). Validate with ICESat-2 and report the bias separately for cells with and without a DSM/lidar epoch.
- **No DSM and no lidar (Fiescher tongue, Zmutt):** the fit is two editions, the balance and `e`; the result has the quality of the editions (stereo, 1 to 3 m) and of the borrowed balance; say so in every label. Do not publish it as "measured".

## 15. Findings of 2026-11-03 (late): wide rock tie and the ICESat-2 bias of Fiescher
- **Wide rock tie** (`params.WIDE_TIE`, now on for every glacier but Aletsch): flights with fewer than 1,500 rock cells in the strip are tied on the rock of the whole grid, offset only, rejected above 5 m. It added flights (Gorner 16 to 24, Findel 15 to 22, Fee 4 to 20) but they cover almost no ice, so the surfaces and the ICESat-2 numbers of those glaciers did not change (median within 0.04 m). It mattered only for Fiescher (full mode instead of reduced) and Corbassière.
- **Fiescher ICESat-2 bias** (+1.52 m median, rmse 3.56 m): the same offset appears where the model equals the lidar. In 2021 the model is the 2021 lidar to better than 1 m, and ICESat-2 still reads 0.9 to 2.5 m lower between 1800 and 3000 m; Aletsch shows the same in 2020/2021 (+2 to +3 m at 2400 to 2800 m). So most of it is an ICESat-2 against swisstopo-surface offset (snow and firn), not a model error. What remains, +1 to +2 m more in 2022 and 2023 above 2600 m, comes from the years after the lidar there: no flight reaches that zone, so it rests on the borrowed Aletsch balance and the fitted ice flow. Restricting the edition-bias estimate to cells observed within a year of the edition (`params.BIAS_WINDOW`) did not change it (1.52 to 1.46 m), so that option is off.
